Your record is the approved account of your career that everything else is built from. CareerBot proposes; you decide.
Nothing enters the record, and nothing is written from it, until you approve it.

## What the record holds

<Terms>
  <Term title="Stories">What you write or say about each job, in your own words. Each save is a version; the record keeps every version. A note (a short story, such as "One more thing about Ironbridge") is read the same way.</Term>
  <Term title="Roles">Employer, title, dates, location and team, one per job. Facts and context are filed under their role.</Term>
  <Term title="Facts">One thing you did or achieved, in one sentence: "Kept the case fill rate above 98% for eight straight quarters while the product line grew by a third." Resume lines, letter paragraphs and answers cite facts.</Term>
  <Term title="Context">Background that helps a fact read right, such as what a company makes or why a number mattered. Context sharpens later rewrites and writing; it isn't a claim of its own.</Term>
  <Term title="Projects">Work in your GitHub repositories, read once you connect GitHub and choose them. A project gives its own facts, stack and skills, and can be linked to the role it was part of.</Term>
  <Term title="Skills">Skills, tools and certifications, gathered from your approved roles, projects and facts when you ask, each named the way resumes and postings write it.</Term>
  <Term title="Career breaks">Time between jobs, with its months and, if you like, a reason. A break can carry facts of its own. Whether it shows on a resume is your choice.</Term>
  <Term title="Insights">What's true of you across roles and projects, each citing the approved facts behind it. They're proposed after a story or project is fully reviewed, and when you ask.</Term>
</Terms>

## Review

Everything proposed waits in <Screen id="review" />, one card at a time. A card says what it is (**New fact**,
**New role**, **Rewrite**, **Question**, **Insight**, **Said twice** and so on), where it came from, and what approving it
does. Facts show the passage of your story they came from.

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| <Kbd>A</Kbd> | **Approve** |
| <Kbd>E</Kbd> | **Edit**: your wording, approved as you save it |
| <Kbd>R</Kbd> | **Reject**, with a reason if you like |
| <Kbd>U</Kbd> | **Undo** the last decision |
| <Kbd>J</Kbd> / <Kbd>K</Kbd> | Next and previous |

Every decision is free and can be undone. Approving or rejecting shows a toast with **Undo**; <Kbd>U</Kbd> does the same.

### Rejecting with a reason

When you reject, you can pick a reason or write your own (for a fact: "Overstates my part", "Wrong role", "Not true").
A reason is kept with the item. It steers later reads, so the same thing isn't proposed again, and later insights learn
from it. A rejected fact stays in your record's lists, marked rejected; **Restore** puts it back to review.

### Other cards

- **Rewrite**: a new wording for a fact you already have. **Use this** takes it, **Edit** puts it in your words, and
  **Keep current** leaves the fact as it is. The other wording stays in the fact's history.
- **Question**: your story and your record disagree (a date, a title, a number). Choose **Story is right**,
  **Record is right** or **Neither** and give the right value. When two jobs at different employers overlap, choose
  **Both are right** if you held both, or fix one job's end or the other's start.
- **Follow-up question**: a question whose answer would sharpen the record. **Answer** it, and your answer becomes a
  rewrite to review or a note read into your record; or choose **Not now**.
- **Said twice**: two facts in one role that say the same thing. Keep one wording, with both facts' sources and history,
  or **Keep both**.
- **Same work?**: a project fact and a role fact that describe the same work. Connected, they're written as one line.

## Approved, then changed

You can change your mind at any time. **Undo approval** takes an item back to review: it stops counting everywhere at
once, and resumes stop using it. Editing an approved fact keeps its earlier wording in its history and marks the lines
written from the old wording (see [Update lines](/docs/how-it-works/writing#when-a-fact-changes)).

## Sources and history

Each fact has **Sources**, the stories or project it came from with the passages it rests on, and **History**, every
wording it has had and how it got there: read from a story, edited by you, or rewritten. A role lists the stories its
facts rest on.

## Reading again

A story is read when you choose **Read**. Once you change it, **Read** reads only what changed since the last read: new
facts, suggested updates to facts already in your record, and facts the story no longer says.

**Read again** reads a story afresh against your record as it is now. It asks what you think was missed first, and only
proposes what isn't in your record yet. You can let it look past what you rejected, for that read only. A project has
**Read again** too, and **Read again with rejected facts**.

Every read uses your AI budget; its estimate shows beside the button.

## What counts

An approved item counts as long as what it rests on still stands:

- A fact or piece of context counts while at least one story it came from isn't rejected. One you added yourself always
  counts.
- A project's facts count only while the project is approved.
- An insight rests on the facts it cites.
- A skill, tool or certification counts while an approved role, project or fact that counts still shows it.

Reject a story and everything that came only from it stops counting at once, everywhere: resumes, letters, ranking and
insights. Restore it and they count again. Deleting a story is different: its facts stay in your record, each marked for
you to **Keep it** or **Reject it**.

Only approved items that count are used to write, rank or position you. Proposals waiting in Review are never used as if
approved.
