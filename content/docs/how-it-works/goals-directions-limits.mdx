Your goals say what you're looking for. CareerBot turns them into two things you approve: directions (the kinds of work
you're going for) and limits (the lines you draw). Together they decide where CareerBot looks, what it hides and how it
ranks.

## Goals are explicit

Three rules hold everywhere:

- Only what you say to avoid is excluded: an industry you avoid, a firm limit, or a past employer. Nothing is left out
  because you didn't mention it.
- A wanted list seeds; it doesn't cap. Industries, company types or titles you want tell CareerBot where to look first
  and what to rank higher. It may add related ones, marked as its suggestion, so you aren't boxed in when you're not sure
  where you fit.
- Every rule that filters or ranks is a setting you can see and switch. They're all listed on this page, under
  [Settings that filter or rank](#settings-that-filter-or-rank).

## The goals story

In <Screen id="goals" />, you write your goals the way you write a job story: what you want next, what you're done with,
pay, place, travel, the companies and work you'd rather avoid. Each save is a new version, and every version is kept.

**Read my goals** reads the newest version against your approved record and proposes limits and directions. They wait in
<Screen id="review" /> and in Goals under **To review**. After you change the story, **Read my goals again** proposes
what's new. A limit or direction you rejected, with why, isn't proposed again.

See [Writing your goals](/docs/best-practices/goals) for how to write it.

## Directions

A direction is one kind of work you're going for, such as "Supply Chain Product" or "Supply Planning". Each has:

<Terms>
  <Term title="Path">How close it is to what you do now: Current (the work of your current role), Adjacent or Stretch. It's used for ranking and positioning only, never as a filter.</Term>
  <Term title="Includes">The related titles or kinds of work it covers.</Term>
  <Term title="Fit with your record">Why your approved record supports it.</Term>
  <Term title="What it is">The work in plain words.</Term>
</Terms>

Approving a direction adds it to your goals: roles are ranked for it, and its positioning can be filled in. Rejecting it
(with why, if you like) keeps it from being proposed again. **Suggest more** proposes kinds of work your approved record
supports that you haven't considered; they wait under **Proposed**, marked "Suggested from your record".

### Positioning

For each approved direction, CareerBot proposes how to present you for it, from your approved record:

- **Positioning**: the case for you in this kind of work.
- **Target titles**: the titles to aim at.
- **Market vocabulary**: the words this market uses for what you've done.
- **Carries over** and **Reframe**: the parts of your record that carry straight over, and the ones to describe in this
  market's terms, each citing approved facts.

Approving the positioning lets resumes for that direction use it. A direction resume can't be written until it's
approved.

### Criteria

The criteria are what company search looks for in this direction: **Titles**, **Industries**, **Company sizes**,
**Stages**, **Keywords** and **Own seed companies** (websites of companies like the ones you want; empty uses your
defaults). Approving them opens company search for the direction. If the criteria name an industry your limits avoid,
the form says so: take it out, or search won't look there.

Once approved, positioning and criteria are never overwritten. **Fill in again** proposes new ones only for the parts you
haven't approved.

## Limits

A limit is one line you draw, in your own sentence, with a rule CareerBot can check against a job posting. Every limit
has a kind:

<Limits />

### How firm

- **Firm** hides roles that fail it.
- **Preference** keeps them, ranked lower.

A role that doesn't say (no pay listed, no travel stated) is never counted against you.

### Where and when it applies

- **Applies to**: every direction (the default), or only the directions you choose. A limit for some directions replaces
  the every-direction limit of the same kind there.
- **Only when**: **Always**, or only **When changing industry** or **When changing kind of work**. A conditional limit
  is an exception that loosens: while its condition holds, a role passes if it meets either the general limit or the
  exception.

Two approved limits of the same kind, condition and directions clash; the form warns you before you save.

### The sentence and the rule

The rule is read from your sentence. If you edit the sentence later, the limit shows "You changed the sentence after this
rule was set", and you can read the rule again from the new wording or keep the rule you have. You can also edit the
rule's values directly. **Add a limit** adds one of your own, approved as you save it.

### What limits do

<Terms>
  <Term title="Location">Roles that name only other countries aren't listed at all, and remote roles only count if your limit allows remote. A role that names no country is kept. Company job counts follow the same area. A role that states it's on-site or hybrid somewhere outside a firm location limit, or remote when you don't work remotely, moves to **Against your limits** in Pursuits with the reason; one that doesn't say how or where is kept.</Term>
  <Term title="Pay">A role that states its pay in US dollars, topping out below a firm limit's minimum (counted by the year), moves to **Against your limits** in Pursuits with the reason. A limit that doesn't say whether it counts base or total compensation counts as base pay. A posted range doesn't say which it is, so a limit on total compensation never sets a role apart. A role with no pay stated, only a starting figure ("from $120k"), or pay in another currency, is kept.</Term>
  <Term title="Seniority, travel and schedule">A role that states a level outside a firm seniority limit, more (or less) travel than a firm travel limit allows, or part-time or full-time against a firm full-time choice, moves to **Against your limits** with the reason. Hours a week aren't stated on roles, so judging weighs them.</Term>
  <Term title="Companies">Industries you avoid are never searched. Employers you exclude, and your past employers, are dropped from every search; a role at an employer a firm limit excludes moves to **Against your limits**. Industries, company sizes and the work itself aren't stated in a role's details, so judging weighs them.</Term>
  <Term title="Eligibility">Checked against what each role states: a role needing a higher clearance than you hold, or not sponsoring a visa when you need one. Against a firm limit, the role moves to **Against your limits** in Pursuits; against a preference, it ranks lower.</Term>
  <Term title="Every limit">Ranking reads your approved limits. A role that fails a firm limit is ranked none; one that misses a preference counts as more of a stretch, while stretch is counted (below).</Term>
</Terms>

Every limit has a switch. Switched off, it's kept and shown, but nothing filters or ranks by it until you switch it on.
**Undo approval** puts it back with the proposed limits; nothing filters by it until you approve it again.

## Settings that filter or rank

These are all the switches that change what you see or the order you see it in.

### Industries you want

In <Screen id="settings" />, under **Companies**, and in Companies under **Lens settings**.

| Choice | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Steer** (the default) | Searches start with your wanted industries and add related ones. A search for companies hiring now is steered into them, added alongside the rest, never narrowing. |
| **Only these** | Searches stay inside your wanted industries, lookalike searches too. |
| **Ignore** | Industry doesn't shape searches. |

Industries you avoid, and your past employers, are excluded whichever you choose.

### Check companies against your goals

The same two places (Lens settings calls it **Judge companies against your goals**). When it's on, filling in a company
also judges it against your approved company goals: fits, partly, doesn't, or can't tell, with a reason. For well-known
companies it may use general knowledge, labelled as such.

| Choice | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Off** | Companies aren't checked against your goals. |
| **Rank** (the default) | Ones that fit your goals rank higher in Found. |
| **Hide** | Ones that don't fit move to **Doesn’t fit your goals**, under Set aside, where you can keep them. |

This judgment is a signal, not a verdict. **Keep anyway** brings a company back, and companies you named yourself are
never set aside. Your own rating is the final say. After you change your goals or directions, **Check all again** judges
every company again, at about a cent for every 40 companies.

### Learn from your ratings

A switch in Settings, under Companies. When on, Targets seed searches for the directions they fit, and companies you said
**Not for me** aren't found again. What you turned down, and why, weighs against similar companies and roles, never as a
limit. It costs about 1 Apollo credit a search for every 5 targets, from the next search and check.

### Job postings from Apollo

A switch in Settings, under Companies. When a company has no public job board, CareerBot asks Apollo for its postings:
about 1 Apollo credit per company, while **Automated Apollo work** is on.

### Sorting roles

In Settings, under **AI**. New roles are sorted into your directions before they're ranked; a role sorted into none of
them isn't ranked and goes under **Other roles** ("Not the work of your directions"). Sorting leans toward including.
You choose what sorts: Jev, a decision model made for this, or the main model.

### Count how big a stretch a role is

In the ⋯ menu on <Screen id="pursuits" />, on by default. When on, a role's level (strong, some, weak or none) still says
only how well the work fits, but its score is lowered for the stretch: years asked beyond your years of work, seniority
well above yours, core requirements your record lacks, and preferences it misses. The stretch is listed with the role in
a few plain words. A stretch lowers a score; it never hides a role. When off, scores stay inside their level and gaps
don't lower them. A change applies to roles ranked from then on; **Rank again** ranks the rest.

### Filters on roles

Pursuits' filters narrow the list you see without changing any ranking: **Score at least**, **Pay at least**, place,
**Work setup**, **Posted within**, **Company** (all, **Targets**, **Maybes** or chosen ones), **Seniority**,
**Years asked**, **Employment type**, **Rating**, **Travel**, **Clearance** and **Visa sponsorship**. A role that doesn't
state something you filter on is kept. **Rating** starts at **All but Not for me**. Firm eligibility limits start the
page with their filters set: the clearance you hold, and only roles that sponsor a visa.

### Your ratings

**Target**, **Maybe** and **Not for me** on a company decide whether its roles are read at all: only Targets and Maybe
companies have their roles read, Targets ranked first. **Not for me** on a role sets it aside with your reason. See
[Companies and roles](/docs/how-it-works/companies-and-roles).
