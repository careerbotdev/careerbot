You say what you want next in one goals story, in your own words: the work, the pay, where, and what to avoid. CareerBot
reads it into directions (kinds of work you're drawn to) and limits (what a role must or should meet). You review both
in <Screen id="goals" /> and <Screen id="review" />. See [Writing your goals](/docs/best-practices/goals).

## The steps

<AiStage stage="goals" />

## Reading your goals

<AiStep id="goalsRead" /> runs when you choose **Read my goals** (or **Read my goals again** after a change). It reads
your goals story against your approved record, directions and limits, and proposes:

- Directions: the kinds of work you say you're drawn to.
- Limits, each as a value in plain words and as filters CareerBot can check: pay, place, seniority and the like. A limit
  is firm when you said it's a must, and a preference otherwise. It may hold for every direction or only some, and
  only in some situations, like a lower level you'd accept to change fields.

A value you didn't give is left out, never filled with a typical one: no pay ceiling you didn't name, no distance you
didn't give. Directions and limits you rejected are passed so they aren't proposed again in any wording, and a limit
resting only on a passage you rejected is skipped. Your approved directions and limits are kept as settled.

Your goals story is read only here. Other steps never see it; they read only the directions and limits you approved.

## Filters from wording

When you edit a limit's wording and leave its filters alone, <AiStep id="limitRule" /> reads that wording, and nothing
else, and sets the limit's filters to match what it now says. It never reads your goals story for it. If you change the
limit again while it runs, its answer isn't saved.

## Filling in directions

<AiStep id="directionsStep" /> runs when you choose **Fill in** on a direction (or **Fill in directions missing
detail** for all of them). For each approved direction it proposes:

- Positioning: how to present your record for this kind of work.
- The titles to aim at, and the words the market uses for the work.
- Which of your stories carry over and which need reframing, each citing approved facts.
- How your past titles translate.
- Search criteria, used to find companies.

**Suggest more** proposes directions you hadn't considered. A suggestion has to rest on approved facts, or it's dropped.

Detail and criteria are reviewed separately, and only approved ones feed resumes and search. What you approved is
never overwritten; directions you rejected or that are still waiting aren't proposed again.

When you change an approved direction's title or summary, roles ranked before the change are marked, so you can rank
them again. See [Roles](/docs/ai/roles).
