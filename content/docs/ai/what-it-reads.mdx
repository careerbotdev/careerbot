Every AI step in CareerBot gets a fixed set of inputs. Each input carries one of five labels, and the label decides what
gets through. The labels are the reason a resume can't cite a fact you never approved, and the reason a step doesn't
keep proposing what you already turned down.

<FilterLabels />

## Approved only

Only what you approved, and only while it still stands. A fact counts while at least one of its sources stands: if you
reject or delete the story a fact came from, and it has no other source, it stops counting everywhere at once. A
project's facts count only while the project itself is approved.

This is the only input that writing ever builds on. Resumes, cover letters, answers, outreach and follow-ups read your
approved roles, facts, projects, context, insights, skills and directions, and nothing proposed or rejected. When a
reply cites anything else, the citation is dropped before it's saved.

## Your words

Your stories, exactly as you wrote them. Reading steps need them as written, because a fact has to quote the story it
came from. When <AiStep id="extract" /> proposes a fact whose quote can't be found in the story being read, the fact is
flagged for you to look at.

Most steps also get your other stories, but for background only: they help the AI understand a passage, never as a
source. Your goals story is never passed as background, and neither is a story you rejected.

<AiStepCard step="extract" />

Two other inputs count as your words:

- A GitHub repository. <AiStep id="readProject" /> reads the repository as it is, the way a story is read: it's
  the evidence, so each fact must cite the files it rests on, and a fact citing no file that was read is flagged.
  Secret-looking files, dependencies, build output, lockfiles and binaries are never fetched, and anything that looks
  like a password, key or token inside what is read is replaced with [redacted] before it goes to the AI.
- What you write when you ask about a role. <AiStep id="ask" /> reads your messages as the question and its context,
  never as a claim about you. If you tell it something new about yourself, that message becomes a note in your own
  words, read like any story, and what it holds arrives in <Screen id="review" /> as proposals.

## Not yet reviewed

Includes proposals you haven't reviewed yet. Steps that get them are told they're waiting for review: not true, and not
to build on. They're passed for three reasons, each a deliberate choice:

- So nothing is proposed twice. <AiStep id="extract" /> and <AiStep id="readProject" /> get what is already
  waiting, so reading a second story doesn't fill Review with the same fact again.
- Because duplicates are usually new. <AiStep id="duplicatesStep" /> reads facts waiting for review alongside
  approved ones, since a duplicate is most often a new proposal repeating an approved fact. Its only output is a flag
  you resolve.
- So waiting items can be tidied. <AiStep id="gatherSkills" /> reads skills, tools and certifications waiting for
  review, so it can rename, regroup or offer them as merges instead of proposing a second copy.

<AiStep id="revision" /> gets every fact the story is a source of, approved and waiting, because those are the facts a
change to the story can affect. It may suggest updates only to those facts.

Set-aside items are never sent.

## Rejected, to skip

Things you rejected, with the reasons you gave, passed only so they aren't proposed again. Reading steps also skip, in
code, a proposal that repeats a rejected fact in the same words. <AiStep id="gatherSkills" /> never brings back a
rejected skill in any spelling.

You can look past your rejections once: **Read again** on a story or project has **Including what I rejected**, which
leaves out that source's own rejections for that read only.

One step reads a rejected fact for a different reason. When you reject a fact that a resume or letter line cites,
<AiStep id="lineUpdate" /> reads its words as what to take out of the line, never as a source.

## Public

Public information about a company or a role: a company's name and website, its public pages and job board, a job
posting. <AiStep id="screening" /> reads only a company's name and website. <AiStep id="enrich" /> reads its site and
public job board. <AiStep id="rankRoles" /> reads the full posting.

## Inputs with no label

A few steps read your own settings or a single thing you wrote, which aren't filtered because they're not record
content:

- <AiStep id="limitRule" /> reads one limit's wording and nothing else, never your goals story.
- Writing steps read the length you set for a resume, and your name from your profile to sign letters and messages.
- <AiStep id="lineCheck" /> reads the line you put in your own words, beside the approved facts it rests on.
