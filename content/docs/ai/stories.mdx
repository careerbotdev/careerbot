Your record starts from your own words. You write a story about one job or project, and CareerBot reads it for roles,
facts and context. Everything it finds comes back as a proposal you approve, edit or reject. Telling a story well takes
time: 20 minutes or more for one job is normal. See [How to tell your story](/docs/best-practices/your-story).

## The steps

<AiStage stage="stories" />

## Reading a story

<AiStep id="extract" /> runs when you choose **Read** on a story in <Screen id="record" /> (or **Done, read it** on your
first story in <Screen id="today" />). It reads that one story against your approved record and proposes roles, facts
and context. Each fact carries the words it came from. What it proposes waits in <Screen id="review" />.

<Shot id="story-read" alt="The Proposals tab of a story after it was read: 4 proposals from version 3, grouped as New facts, Roles and Context, each with its status and Built on." />

It takes facts only from the story being read. Your other stories are there to help it understand, not as sources, and
a fact whose quoted words can't be found in this story is flagged for you to look at. It is told never to invent a
customer, number, result or skill.

A role's title is the official one only when your story says it. When it doesn't, the role keeps what you call the
job as another title, and resumes use that until you give the official title; the role and its card in Review say so.
Time away from paid work (caring for someone, a move, study) is proposed as a career break, never as a job at an
employer named after what you did; side projects from that time are named on the break, and their facts sit on it.
A number you only guessed at ("I think it was about an hour") stays an estimate in the fact, and in every resume and
letter written from it.

A fact you already have in the same words, in the same role or project, isn't proposed twice: the existing fact gains
this story as another source. Facts waiting in Review and facts you rejected are passed so it doesn't repeat them.

## Reading the changes

When you change a story that was read before and choose **Read** again, <AiStep id="revision" /> reads only what
changed since the version it last read. It may propose new facts, suggest updates to facts the change affects, and flag
facts the story no longer says. It touches only facts this story is a source of, and an approved fact changes only when
you accept the update.

To read a whole story again against your record as it is now, use **Read again**. Tick **Including what I rejected** to
look past what you rejected from that story, for that read only.

## Rewriting a fact

<AiStep id="rework" /> runs when you choose **Add context or rewrite** on a fact, or **Ask for a rewrite** in Review.
You say what to change or add; your note is the final word. It reads the fact, its history, its role's approved context
and the story it came from, and suggests new wording. The suggestion waits beside the fact until you use it. Details
from your note that don't fit the wording are kept as context on the fact.

## Follow-up questions

<AiStep id="followups" /> runs only when you choose **Suggest questions** in Review. It reads your approved record and
asks a few short questions where an answer would sharpen it: a missing number, the scope of something you led, what
came of a project.

An answer about one fact becomes a rewrite suggestion on that fact. Any other answer becomes a short note, read like a
story, so what it holds comes back as proposals. Choose **Not now** on a question and it isn't asked again.

## Checking for disagreements

<AiStep id="check" /> runs when you choose **Check for disagreements** in Record, or **Look for conflicts** in Review.
It compares every story with your approved record and the answers you've given, and asks which is right wherever they
differ: dates, titles, employers, numbers. It never picks for you. Your record doesn't change until you answer.

Two jobs in your record at different employers whose dates overlap by three months or more are asked about too, without
AI, whenever a role is approved or changed: **Both are right** when you held both (part-time, volunteer or freelance
work beside a job), or fix one job's end or the other's start.
