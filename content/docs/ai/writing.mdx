Everything CareerBot writes under your name is written from your approved record, and nothing else. Each line cites the
approved facts it rests on; a citation of anything else is dropped. CareerBot never sends anything: you send letters
and messages yourself, from your own email.

## The steps

<AiStage stage="writing" />

## Resumes

<AiStep id="resume" /> writes resumes at three levels:

- The base resume, neutral: **Write** (then **Write again**) in <Screen id="resumes" />.
- One for each direction, from that direction's approved positioning: **Write the resume** (then **Rewrite**) on a
  direction in <Screen id="goals" />.
- One tailored to a role: **Tailor a resume** on a role or a pursuit in <Screen id="pursuits" />. If the direction has
  no resume yet, it's written first, in the same run.

Each has a summary, experience with bullets, and skills. Bullets cite approved facts; a line left with no approved fact
behind it is marked **No approved basis**. Skills come only from the skills you approved. Approved career breaks get an
entry of their own. A resume is written to the length you set.

A tailored resume also maps the posting's requirements to your record, each graded strong, partial or thin. A
requirement called strong with no approved fact behind it is at most partial. It may suggest, for a past role, a title
your facts support in the posting's words; it's only a suggestion.

Every version is kept. When your record changes after a resume was written, **Add what’s new** writes lines for what's
new, and **Update all** writes new versions for you to keep or discard.

## Cover letters

<AiStep id="letter" /> runs when you choose **Write a cover letter** in a pursuit. It writes in the first person, from
your approved record, the resume tailored to the role (the one you sent, once sent) and the posting, and signs with
your name from your profile. Paragraphs cite approved facts.

It won't write one before you've tailored a resume to the role: "Tailor your resume to this role first." Once the
pursuit is sent, the letter stays as it was sent. Your own edit is kept as a new version; a paragraph you kept word for
word keeps its facts.

## Ask about this role

<AiStep id="ask" /> answers in a conversation kept with each pursuit. Paste an application question and you get an
answer to paste back, in the first person; you can also ask about the role, the company, or how to present yourself.
It rests on your approved record, the resume tailored to the role and the posting, and says when your record doesn't
support something. One question at a time: "Wait for the answer to the last one."

If your message tells it something new about you, it doesn't treat that as true. Your message becomes a note in your own
words, read like a story, and what it holds arrives in <Screen id="review" /> as proposals. **Keep** saves an answer to
the pursuit under the question it answered.

## Outreach and follow-ups

<AiStep id="outreach" /> runs when you choose **Draft a message** for a contact at the pursuit's company. It writes a
short outreach message, subject and text, from your approved record and the role, signed with your name: who you are,
why this role, why you fit, and one small ask that suits the contact's group. It knows whether you applied and who else
you already wrote to there, so a second contact gets a new message, never a copy.

<AiStep id="followUpWriter" /> writes a short follow-up when an application goes quiet or after an interview: from a
reminder in <Screen id="today" />, or **Write follow-up** in the pursuit. It goes to the person you last wrote to, else
the likely hiring manager, and reads where your application stands: status, dates and what was already sent. A rewrite
waits beside the draft until you keep it.

Both need approved facts first: "Approve some facts first; messages are written only from your approved record." Neither
sends anything. Choose **Copy and open Mail** to send it from your own email, then **Mark as sent**. A follow-up marked
sent goes on the pursuit's timeline and the next reminder counts from that day.

## Checking your own words

When you put a resume line or the summary in your own words, <AiStep id="lineCheck" /> runs only when you choose
**Check against facts**, with its cost beside the button. It reads your words beside the approved facts the line rests
on and answers **Supported**, or **Goes beyond** with the words that go further than the facts. It changes nothing.
Edit the words again and the answer is cleared.

## When a fact changes

When you edit or reject an approved fact after a document was written, the lines citing it are found on every document
in use: the base resume, each direction's current resume, and a tailored resume, cover letter and answers of a pursuit
not yet sent. **Update lines** shows its cost; <AiStep id="lineUpdate" /> then rewrites just those lines from the facts
as they read now. A line left resting on no fact that counts is dropped.

The new lines wait for you to **Apply** or **Discard**. A line you put in your own words stays yours unless you tick
**Use the new line**. If a fact changed again since the new lines were written, Apply changes nothing: "A fact changed
since these lines were written. Update them again." A document sent with a pursuit is never changed.
