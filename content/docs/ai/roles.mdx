CareerBot reads every open role at your Target and Maybe companies from their own job boards: each morning, and when
you choose **Check roles again** in <Screen id="pursuits" />. A role gone from a complete read of its board is closed.
Then each new role goes through two AI steps: a quick sort, then a full ranking. The point is to tell you which roles
deserve your time, not to predict whether you'll be hired.

## The steps

<AiStage stage="roles" />

## Sort, then rank

Ranking reads a role in full against a direction, so it costs more than a glance. Sorting comes first to keep that
cost down.

<AiStep id="sortRoles" /> decides which of your approved directions each role could plausibly be for, or none. It leans
toward including: a role wrongly left out is never read again, while an extra one only costs a ranking. Only the
directions a role is sorted to are ranked, and a role sorted to none isn't ranked at all. Those roles stay in the list,
under **Other roles**.

Sorting waits until every new role's description is in. It reads each description without the text a company repeats
across its postings (the same benefits and company blurb on every role), so the role's own work stands out.

You choose how roles are sorted in <Screen id="settings" />, AI, under **Advanced**: with a chat model (the main one,
or one you give the Sorting roles task), or with Jev, a decision model made for this, which answers for each direction
whether the role's main work is that direction's work. See [Models and AI tasks](/docs/ai/models).

<AiStep id="rankRoles" /> then reads each role against the directions it was sorted to, from the whole description,
with what its board states and your approved directions, limits, roles, facts and projects. For each direction it gives
a level, a score and one plain sentence of reason. When a posting is too thin to judge, the reason starts with "Read
this one yourself:" and says what's missing.

## Levels

| Level | What it means | Score band |
| --- | --- | --- |
| strong | The work this direction is about, not beneath your level | 75 to 100 |
| some | Plausible and worth a look | 50 to 74 |
| weak | Only partly this direction's work | 25 to 49 |
| none | Different work, or against a firm limit | 0 to 24 |

## What the score means

The score, from 0 to 100, says how worth your time a role is. A role is listed under its best direction, best score
first.

With **Count how big a stretch a role is** on (it is unless you turn it off, in the menu in Pursuits), the level still
says only how well the work fits. The score starts inside its level's band, then drops for each gap between the role
and you: more years asked than you've worked (counted from your approved roles' dates, career breaks left out),
seniority well above yours, core requirements your record lacks, and preferences it misses. A big stretch can take a
score below its band. Each role lists its stretch in short plain words. A stretch lowers a score; it never hides a
role.

With it off, the score only places the role inside its level's band, and gaps in your background never lower it.

A firm limit makes a role none. Roles that state something against a firm eligibility limit, like a security clearance
above the one you hold or no visa sponsorship when you need it, are set apart under **Against your limits**. So are roles
outside a firm location limit: on-site or hybrid somewhere you won't work, or remote when you don't work remotely, with
the reason ("On-site in San Francisco; you work hybrid from Denver or remote"). A role that doesn't say how or where
it's done is kept.

## What else it reads from a role

Ranking also writes, for each role:

- Its details as the description states them: pay, setup, locations, seniority, years asked, employment type, travel,
  clearance and visa. Never guessed. What the job board states in its own fields wins over what ranking read.
- A brief: the job in two or three neutral sentences from the posting, and why it fits you and what's thin, from your
  approved record only.

These are what the role filters in Pursuits work on.

## Ranking again

Nothing is ranked again by itself. When you change an approved direction's title or summary, roles ranked before the
change are marked, and **Rank again** ranks them for your directions as they are now. It reads no job boards, and shows
what it costs before you start.

A role whose own input fails three times in a pass is set aside until the next pass. If this month's AI budget runs out,
the pass pauses and picks up where it left off. See [Budgets and costs](/docs/ai/budgets).
