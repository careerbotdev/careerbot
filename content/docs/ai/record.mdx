Once you've approved facts, a few steps read across them: to say what's true of you across jobs, to list your skills
once, to catch facts said twice, and to add your GitHub projects. Each proposes; nothing in your record changes until
you decide in <Screen id="record" /> or <Screen id="review" />.

## The steps

<AiStage stage="record" />

## Insights

<AiStep id="insights" /> reads across your approved roles, facts, context, directions and projects, the way a career
editor would, and proposes things that are true of you across jobs that you may never have put into words. Each insight
cites the approved facts it rests on; an insight left with no approved fact behind it is dropped. A project's facts
count as evidence just like a role's.

It runs on its own, quietly, once a story or project is fully reviewed: nothing left from it to approve or reject. It
also runs when you choose **Find new insights**. It needs at least two approved facts. It never brings back an insight
you rejected, and doesn't repeat one you already have or one waiting for review.

## Skills, tools and certifications

<AiStep id="gatherSkills" /> runs only when you ask: **Gather** on Skills and tools in Record. It reads your approved
roles' skills and tools, your approved projects' stacks and your approved facts, and proposes each skill, tool and
certification once, named the way resumes and postings name it, with where your record shows it. Vague ones are
flagged as low value, and near-duplicates are offered as merges. It never merges anything you approved, and never pairs
two items you kept apart. A rejected skill is never proposed again, in any spelling.

Resumes list only the skills you approved here.

## Duplicates

<AiStep id="duplicatesStep" /> runs only when you choose **Find duplicates** in Record, or **Find facts said twice**
in Review. It looks within each role for facts that say the same thing, often one from each of two stories, and flags
them. Nothing changes until you choose: **Merge** keeps one wording with both facts' sources, history and notes;
**Keep both** keeps them apart, and that pair is never flagged again. A new search replaces the last one's flags.

## Same work

When a project is linked to a role, its facts and the role's facts often tell the same work twice: once from the
repository, once from your story. <AiStep id="sameWorkStep" /> runs when you link a project to a role, and when you
choose **Find same work**. It reads the approved facts of the project and of that role and suggests pairs that describe
the same work, the richer one as the lead.

**Connect** links the two facts without touching their words: resumes then write one line for them, from the lead, with
the other as context. **Keep separate** means that pair is never suggested again. Unlinking or relinking the project
drops the suggestions still waiting.

## Reading a project

<AiStep id="readProject" /> reads one of your GitHub repositories after you connect GitHub in Record and choose which
repositories to read. Each repository is read whole, not just its code: the README, docs, planning and design files,
changelogs, the stack from its manifests, a small sample of code and the commit history. It can open that one
repository and nothing else.

Secret-looking files, like env files, keys and certificates, are never fetched, and anything that looks like a
password, key or token inside what is read is redacted before it goes to the AI. A large repository is read in parts,
then the facts from those parts are merged into at most six.

It proposes the project and facts about it, each citing the files it came from; a fact citing no file it read is
flagged. It is told to write plainly: you built it, tools are tools, no inflated labels. The project and its facts wait
in Review. Only approved ones reach insights, directions, role ranking and resumes. **Read again** on a project reads it
against your record as it is now, and keeps what you approved or wrote.
